# Ship Tracks & Global Temperature — Overview

> Evolving synthesis of everything in the wiki. Updated by wiki-ingest when sources shift the understanding.

## Current Understanding

**The phenomenon.** [[ship-tracks]] — bright cloud lines along shipping routes, discovered in 1966 satellite imagery — exist because ship-exhaust aerosol seeds marine low clouds with extra droplets, brightening them ([[twomey-effect]]). Automated censuses show they were ~10× more common than manual surveys found, and plume-tracking shows ships alter clouds substantially even where no track is visible ([[manshausen-2022-invisible-ship-tracks]]). Pre-2020 shipping sulfur was providing real planetary cooling: the observationally informed estimate for low-cloud aerosol forcing is −1.0 W/m² globally, with shipping corridors locally blocking ~2 W/m² ([[diamond-2020-shipping-corridor]]).

**The experiment.** The [[imo-2020-regulation]] cut marine fuel sulfur ~86% overnight in January 2020. Visible ship tracks collapsed to record lows ([[yuan-2022-ship-track-climatology]]); corridor cloud microphysics went measurably quiet ([[diamond-2023-imo2020-corridor]]). This is the cleanest large-scale aerosol-removal experiment ever run — "inadvertent geoengineering, terminated" ([[inadvertent-geoengineering]]).

**The number.** Removing the sulfur produced a positive (warming) radiative forcing. Three independent climate models cluster tightly at +0.12–0.14 W/m² globally; observation-led estimates straddle that range (+0.074 regional-only to +0.2 ocean-only). Full comparison: [[imo2020-forcing-estimates]]. Model temperature response: ~+0.05°C over the 2020s — about 2–3 years of global warming pulled forward — amplified toward the Arctic ([[jordan-henry-2024-ukesm1]], [[yoshioka-2024-warming-effects]]).

**The controversy.** Whether that forcing meaningfully caused the record 2023–24 warmth is unresolved ([[2023-warmth-attribution]]). Hansen's camp says yes — half the unexpected spike, with IPCC-class models underestimating [[aerosol-masking]] and climate sensitivity ([[hansen-2025-acceleration]]). The modeling consensus says it contributed at most ~0.08 K of a ~0.2 K anomaly, with the response mostly still to come. Satellite energy-budget work confirms the 2023 anomaly came via record-low cloud albedo but can't yet split aerosols from variability from an emerging cloud feedback ([[goessling-2024-albedo]]).

**The uncertainty engine.** Nearly all disagreement traces to [[cloud-adjustments]] — what liquid water path and cloud fraction do after droplet number changes. Visible-track studies say adjustments are small; invisible-track studies say strongly cooling; Yuan's decomposition makes cloud fraction 60% of the forcing.

**The future application.** The same physics underlies deliberate [[marine-cloud-brightening]]. IMO 2020's chief lesson for it is chastening: a known, fleet-scale perturbation was barely detectable (12–40% regional detection rates, [[zhang-2025-hard-to-detect]]), so verifying deliberate intervention will be hard ([[feingold-2024-mcb-roadmap]]).

## Open Questions

- What are the signs and magnitudes of LWP and cloud-fraction adjustments by cloud regime? (The single biggest lever — [[cloud-adjustments]].)
- How much of the 2023–24 planetary albedo decline is aerosol unmasking vs internal variability vs an emerging low-cloud feedback? ([[goessling-2024-albedo]])
- Is transient response to the IMO 2020 forcing fast (energy-balance view, Yuan/Hansen) or lagged (coupled-model view)? A longer post-2020 record decides.
- Will the proposed IMO2020 model intercomparison happen, and does it narrow the +0.07–0.2 W/m² spread? ([[jordan-henry-2024-ukesm1]])
- Does higher implied aerosol cooling force ECS upward, as [[hansen-2023-pipeline]] and [[manshausen-2022-invisible-ship-tracks]] both suggest by different routes?
- Can MCB-scale injections be made detectable/verifiable given the shipping experience? ([[zhang-2025-hard-to-detect]], [[feingold-2024-mcb-roadmap]])
