# Analysis: Did IMO 2020 Cause the 2023–24 Warmth?

The field's live controversy. 2023 blew past the previous global temperature record by 0.17 K, reaching +1.48 K over pre-industrial; standard drivers (greenhouse forcing + El Niño) leave roughly 0.2 K unexplained.[^1] The North Atlantic — exactly where shipping forcing peaks — ran hottest. Three camps:

## Camp 1: Ships did it (Hansen, Yuan)

[[hansen-2025-acceleration]] attributes about half of the unexpected two-year 0.4°C jump to the [[imo-2020-regulation]] aerosol cut, as the arrival of the [[aerosol-masking]] debt his 2023 paper predicted.[^2] [[yuan-2024-termination-shock]] provides the mechanism-level support: +0.2 W/m² ocean forcing, ~80% of the post-2020 increase in planetary heat uptake, North Atlantic peak forcing of 1.4 W/m².[^3] Both rely on energy-balance reasoning with fast transient response.

## Camp 2: Ships contributed a slice (the modeling consensus)

Coupled-model studies find the shipping signal real but small-and-slow: [[jordan-henry-2024-ukesm1]] — IMO2020 "helps to explain the exceptional warming in 2023, but other factors are needed to fully account for it," with 2023's record-low reflected shortwave made *more likely* yet still within no-IMO2020 ensemble variability.[^4] [[yoshioka-2024-warming-effects]] bounds the possible 2023 contribution at ≤0.08 K of the 0.10–0.12 K unexplained.[^5] [[hausfather-forster-2023-carbon-brief]] argued in real time that by 2023 only hundredths of a degree could have materialized — far short of a spike ~0.2°C above record.[^6] [[gettelman-2024-brought-forward]] sits here too: spatial correlations are suggestive, robust attribution absent.[^7]

## Camp 3: Look at the clouds, cause unknown

[[goessling-2024-albedo]] confirms the energy went missing via record-low planetary albedo from declining northern low clouds — but declines to name the cause, listing internal variability, aerosol reductions, and an emerging low-cloud feedback as open candidates.[^8] If the feedback explanation wins, 2023 is scarier than the shipping story (it would imply higher sensitivity); if variability wins, the anomaly partly relaxes.

## Scorecard as of mid-2026

Established: IMO 2020 produced a real positive forcing (~+0.07–0.2 W/m²) with a North Atlantic bullseye ([[imo2020-forcing-estimates]]). Contested: whether the fast temperature response is large enough to matter for 2023 specifically. The distinguishing evidence — longer post-2020 record, a model intercomparison ([[jordan-henry-2024-ukesm1]] proposed one), better constrained [[cloud-adjustments]] — is mostly still pending. El Niño's exit without temperatures fully relaxing has kept Camp 1 alive.
