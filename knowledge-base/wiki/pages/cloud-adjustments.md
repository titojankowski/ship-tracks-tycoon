# Cloud Adjustments (LWP and Cloud Fraction)

## Description

Everything a cloud does *after* the [[twomey-effect]]: with more, smaller droplets, precipitation can be suppressed (cloud retains water and lives longer → more cooling), or evaporation and entrainment can increase (cloud thins → less cooling, possibly warming). The two bookkeeping terms are the liquid water path (LWP) adjustment and the cloud-fraction adjustment. Their signs and magnitudes are the central unresolved question of aerosol forcing — and the reason published IMO-2020 estimates disagree by a factor of ~3 (see [[imo2020-forcing-estimates]]).

The evidence is genuinely split. Visible-corridor observations found LWP adjustments "small or countervailing" — the Twomey term dominates.[^1] Plume-tracking of invisible tracks found the opposite regime: weakly positive LWP responses that, scaled globally, give an LWP-adjustment forcing of −0.76 (−1.03, −0.49) W/m² versus the IPCC's +0.2 ± 0.2 W/m² — a sign disagreement with the assessed value, attributed to meteorological selection bias in visible-track studies.[^2] Meanwhile [[yuan-2024-termination-shock]]'s decomposition makes cloud-fraction adjustment the *largest* term (~60%) of the IMO-2020 forcing, with LWP near zero.[^3]

[[diamond-2023-imo2020-corridor]] names cloud macrophysical adjustments as the key remaining unknown,[^4] and for [[marine-cloud-brightening]] the same uncertainty decides whether spraying brightens clouds or accidentally thins them.[^5]
