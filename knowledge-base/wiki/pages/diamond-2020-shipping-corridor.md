# Diamond et al. 2020 — Substantial Cloud Brightening From Shipping in Subtropical Low Clouds

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2019AV000111 (AGU Advances 1, e2019AV000111) — local copy: `raw/diamond-2020-shipping-corridor.pdf`
**Date ingested:** 2026-08-09
**Type:** paper

## Summary

The first detection of a climatologically relevant cloud radiative effect from shipping. Earlier work could see individual [[ship-tracks]] but not a regional-scale forcing; models predicted a sizable negative forcing that observations couldn't confirm.[^1] Diamond and colleagues exploited a natural experiment: the southeast Atlantic shipping corridor cuts through one of the planet's great stratocumulus decks, and prevailing winds keep ship emissions confined near the corridor. Using universal kriging, they built a statistical counterfactual — what the clouds would look like with no shipping — from surrounding unpolluted clouds, and attributed a significant reflectivity increase inside the corridor to enhanced cloud droplet number.[^2]

Two results anchor the field. First, the brightening is dominated by the [[twomey-effect]] (more, smaller droplets), with small or even countervailing liquid-water-path adjustments — an observational data point in the long-running [[cloud-adjustments]] debate.[^3] Second, scaling the corridor result to global low clouds yields an observationally informed effective radiative forcing of −1.0 W/m² (−1.6 to −0.4 W/m²) — a large cooling that would mean aerosols mask roughly a third of greenhouse warming.[^4]

This paper is the "before" picture for the IMO 2020 story: it quantified how much cooling ship sulfur was providing, three years before [[diamond-2023-imo2020-corridor]] measured the same corridor going dim after the [[imo-2020-regulation]].

## Key Takeaways

- First observation of ship-induced cloud brightening at climate-relevant spatial and temporal scales.[^1]
- Method: universal kriging counterfactual for the southeast Atlantic corridor (the approach reused in the 2023 follow-up).[^2]
- Brightening driven by droplet number; LWP adjustments small or offsetting.[^3]
- Corridor clouds blocked roughly 2 W/m² of sunlight locally.[^5]
- Global low-cloud aerosol ERF estimated at −1.0 W/m² (−1.6 to −0.4), about one-third the magnitude of greenhouse forcing.[^4][^5]
