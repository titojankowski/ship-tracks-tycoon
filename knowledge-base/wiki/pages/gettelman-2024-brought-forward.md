# Gettelman et al. 2024 — Has Reducing Ship Emissions Brought Forward Global Warming?

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2024GL109077 (Geophysical Research Letters 51) — local copy: `raw/gettelman-2024-brought-forward.pdf`
**Date ingested:** 2026-08-09
**Type:** paper

## Summary

The closest thing to a community-consensus paper on the [[imo-2020-regulation]]'s climate effect: its author list unites nearly every group in this wiki — Gettelman (PNNL), Christensen, Diamond ([[diamond-2020-shipping-corridor]]), Gryspeerdt, Manshausen ([[manshausen-2022-invisible-ship-tracks]]), Stier, Watson-Parris, Yoshioka ([[yoshioka-2024-warming-effects]]), and Yuan ([[yuan-2024-termination-shock]]).[^1]

Model simulations of the ~80% fuel-sulfur cut give a net radiative forcing of +0.12 W/m² since 2020 — the middle of the published range.[^2] The observational hook: Northern Hemisphere surface-temperature anomalies in 2022–2023 correlate with observed cloud radiative forcing, which in turn is spatially correlated with the simulated shipping forcing — circumstantial but suggestive evidence that "shipping emissions changes could be accelerating global warming."[^3]

The paper is deliberately hedged on attribution and closes with an argument that matters beyond shipping: if we can't robustly attribute a known, sizeable aerosol perturbation, deliberate [[marine-cloud-brightening]] would face the same attribution problem — "the difficulty in robust attribution highlights the large uncertainty in attributing proposed deliberate climate intervention."[^4]

## Key Takeaways

- Net radiative forcing from IMO 2020: +0.12 W/m² (model simulations).[^2]
- 2022–23 NH temperature anomalies spatially correlate with observed and simulated cloud forcing patterns.[^3]
- Needs better ship-position data and emission understanding to constrain further.[^5]
- Explicit bridge from inadvertent (shipping) to deliberate ([[marine-cloud-brightening]]) intervention attribution.[^4]
