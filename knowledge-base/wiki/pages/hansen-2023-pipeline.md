# Hansen et al. 2023 — Global warming in the pipeline

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfclm/kgad008 (Oxford Open Climate Change 3, kgad008) — local copy: `raw/hansen-2023-pipeline.pdf`
**Date ingested:** 2026-08-09
**Type:** paper

## Summary

The maximalist framing of [[aerosol-masking]], from James Hansen's group. Its relevance to this wiki is the argument that aerosol cooling — and therefore climate sensitivity — is understated in IPCC best estimates, and that the decline of aerosol emissions since ~2010 (accelerated by the [[imo-2020-regulation]]) is now unmasking that hidden warming. The paper's own numbers: equilibrium climate sensitivity of 1.2 ± 0.3°C per W/m², i.e. 4.8 ± 1.2°C for doubled CO₂ — well above the IPCC's ~3°C best estimate.[^1]

On warming rate, it predicts that the aerosol decline "should increase the 1970–2010 global warming rate of 0.18°C per decade to a post-2010 rate of at least 0.27°C per decade."[^2] It explicitly names shipping-fuel restrictions as "the great inadvertent aerosol experiment" — a real-world test of aerosol forcing that satellite-era energy-balance measurements (CERES/EEI) can read out.[^3] On trajectory, it projects 1.5°C exceeded in the 2020s and 2°C before 2050 under current policy.[^4]

This paper is contested. Its high sensitivity and large aerosol forcing sit outside the assessed IPCC ranges, and mainstream commentators (e.g. Michael Mann, Gavin Schmidt) pushed back on the acceleration claims — context that matters when weighing it against the moderate model results of [[jordan-henry-2024-ukesm1]] and [[yoshioka-2024-warming-effects]]. It remains foundational because the ship-track literature after 2023 is largely a referendum on whether Hansen's aerosol numbers are right. See [[2023-warmth-attribution]].

## Key Takeaways

- ECS claim: 4.8 ± 1.2°C per CO₂ doubling — far above IPCC best estimate.[^1]
- Post-2010 warming rate predicted ≥0.27°C/decade (vs 0.18 for 1970–2010) due to aerosol decline.[^2]
- Coins the "great inadvertent aerosol experiment" framing for IMO 2020.[^3]
- The Faustian-bargain view: aerosol cleanup extracts payment for decades of masked warming.[^5]
