# Hansen et al. 2025 — Global warming has accelerated: Are the United Nations and the public well-informed?

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1080/00139157.2025.2434494 (Environment: Science and Policy for Sustainable Development 67(1), 6–44); abstract: https://www.giss.nasa.gov/pubs/abs/ha05220r.html
**Date ingested:** 2026-08-09
**Type:** paper

## Summary

Hansen's follow-through on [[hansen-2023-pipeline]], now with the 2023–24 temperature spike in hand. Global temperature jumped more than 0.4°C in two years, reaching about +1.6°C above the 1880–1920 baseline by August 2024 — roughly twice the warming expected from the weak 2023–24 El Niño.[^1]

The central attribution claim: approximately half of that unexpected warming came from reduced ship aerosol emissions under the [[imo-2020-regulation]], whose cooling effect the authors argue was underestimated in IPCC-class models.[^2] This is the strongest published attribution of recent warming to shipping — well beyond what [[jordan-henry-2024-ukesm1]] ("helps to explain… but other factors are needed") or [[hausfather-forster-2023-carbon-brief]] (hundredths of a degree by 2023) support. The satellite-albedo analysis of [[goessling-2024-albedo]] agrees a ~0.2 K albedo-driven gap exists but attributes it mostly to low-cloud decline of uncertain cause.

Downstream claims escalate: temperatures will oscillate near/above +1.5°C, and — if sensitivity and forcing are as claimed — AMOC shutdown becomes "likely within the next 20-30 years" absent intervention.[^3] These conclusions are contested in the community but define one pole of the live debate tracked in [[2023-warmth-attribution]].

## Key Takeaways

- +0.4°C spike over two years; +1.6°C peak vs 1880–1920; twice the expected El Niño response.[^1]
- ~Half of the unexpected warming attributed to the IMO 2020 ship-aerosol cut.[^2]
- Claims IPCC underestimates both aerosol forcing and climate sensitivity; warns of AMOC shutdown in 20–30 years.[^2][^3]
