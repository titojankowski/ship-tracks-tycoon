# Hausfather & Forster 2023 — How low-sulphur shipping rules are affecting global warming (Carbon Brief)

**Source:** https://www.carbonbrief.org/analysis-how-low-sulphur-shipping-rules-are-affecting-global-warming (2023-07-03)
**Date ingested:** 2026-08-09
**Type:** article (analysis by Zeke Hausfather and Piers Forster)

## Summary

The go-to moderate synthesis, written in July 2023 as the North Atlantic spike had everyone pointing at ships. Hausfather and Forster survey the pre-existing literature (Lauer 2007, Fuglestvedt 2009, Partanen 2013, Sofiev 2018, Jin 2018, Bilsback 2020, Yuan 2022, Forster 2023) — published forcing estimates for shipping-sulfur changes ranging from ~0 to 0.3 W/m² — and produce their own central estimate of 0.079 W/m² for the [[imo-2020-regulation]].[^1]

Their bottom line on temperature: about 0.05°C of extra warming by 2050 — "equivalent to approximately two additional years of emissions."[^2] For 2023 specifically they invoke thermal inertia: less than half the equilibrium response would have materialized by 2023, "likely only in the hundredths of a degree globally" — far too small to explain a sea-surface-temperature spike running ~0.2°C above prior records.[^3]

The framing sentence most often quoted since: the shift to low-sulphur fuel "may increase the warming we experience in the near future, but it will not change our long-term trajectory."[^4]

## Key Takeaways

- Literature range ~0–0.3 W/m²; their own estimate 0.079 W/m².[^1]
- ~0.05°C extra warming by 2050; hundredths of a degree by 2023.[^2][^3]
- Explicitly rejects IMO 2020 as the main driver of the mid-2023 SST spike.[^3]
