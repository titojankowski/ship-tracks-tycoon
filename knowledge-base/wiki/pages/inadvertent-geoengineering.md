# Inadvertent Geoengineering & Termination Shock

## Description

The reframing that made the shipping story legible: for decades, sulfur-rich marine fuel operated as an unintentional [[marine-cloud-brightening]] program along the world's shipping lanes, cooling the planet without anyone deciding to. The [[imo-2020-regulation]] then functioned as a *termination* of that program — abrupt, global, and unplanned. "Termination shock" is borrowed from the geoengineering literature, where it names the rapid warming that follows if a solar-radiation-management scheme stops suddenly; [[yuan-2024-termination-shock]] applied it to IMO 2020 explicitly.[^1] Hansen's version of the same idea is "the great inadvertent aerosol experiment."[^2]

The framing earns its keep in three ways. First, it sets expectations: termination of a sustained negative forcing produces fast transient warming concentrated where the forcing was — North Atlantic and North Pacific shipping lanes.[^3] Second, it makes shipping a dry run for deliberate intervention: the struggle to *attribute* even this known perturbation quantifies how hard verifying deliberate MCB would be.[^4] Third, it sharpens the policy dilemma — the regulation saved lives via air quality while measurably warming the climate, a trade-off now posed openly to policymakers.[^5]

The caution: "termination shock" imports an implication of large, fast warming that the moderate estimates ([[jordan-henry-2024-ukesm1]], [[hausfather-forster-2023-carbon-brief]]) don't support — 2–3 years of pulled-forward warming is real but not a shock. The term is as much rhetoric as physics; see [[2023-warmth-attribution]] for how the two camps use it.
