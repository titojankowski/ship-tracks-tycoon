# Jordan & Henry 2024 — IMO2020 Regulations Accelerate Global Warming by up to 3 Years in UKESM1

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2024EF005011 (Earth's Future 12) — local copy: `raw/jordan-henry-2024-ukesm1.pdf`
**Date ingested:** 2026-08-09
**Type:** paper

## Summary

The cleanest single-model quantification of the [[imo-2020-regulation]]. Jordan and Henry ran paired UKESM1 experiments — with and without the sulfur cut. Fixed-SST atmosphere-only runs isolate the forcing: a global effective radiative forcing of 0.139 ± 0.019 W/m², most of it from aerosol-induced cloud changes rather than direct aerosol effects.[^1] Coupled ocean-atmosphere runs then give the temperature response: +0.046 ± 0.010°C on average over 2020–2029, which they translate into the paper's title metric — "approximately 2–3 years of global warming" pulled forward.[^2]

The microphysical fingerprint in the model matches the observations elsewhere in this wiki: significant droplet-number and droplet-size changes over high-traffic regions, translating to reduced cloud albedo in the North Atlantic and North Pacific.[^3]

On the 2023 question they are carefully split-the-difference: IMO2020 "helps to explain the exceptional warming in 2023, but other factors are needed to fully account for it," and while 2023's record drop in reflected shortwave was made *more likely* by the regulation, the observations remain within the variability of the counterfactual ensemble.[^4] They close by calling for a model intercomparison project.[^5]

## Key Takeaways

- ERF = 0.139 ± 0.019 W/m² (fixed-SST UKESM1), dominated by cloud changes.[^1]
- Warming: +0.046 ± 0.010°C over 2020–2029 ≈ 2–3 years of global warming brought forward.[^2]
- Cloud-albedo decrease concentrated in North Atlantic and North Pacific.[^3]
- 2023: contributes but cannot fully explain; record-low reflected shortwave made more likely but not attributable.[^4]
