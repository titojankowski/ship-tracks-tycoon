# Twomey Effect

## Description

The first aerosol indirect effect, proposed by Sean Twomey in the 1970s: for a fixed amount of cloud liquid water, adding aerosol (cloud condensation nuclei) produces more, smaller droplets, which increases total droplet surface area and therefore cloud albedo. Brighter clouds reflect more sunlight — a negative (cooling) radiative forcing. It is the textbook mechanism behind [[ship-tracks]]: Twomey identified it shortly after Conover's track discovery as the explanation for the anomalously bright lines.[^1]

In the shipping context the Twomey effect is well-confirmed and the *least* controversial term. The corridor observations of [[diamond-2020-shipping-corridor]] found brightening "dominated by an increase in the number of cloud droplets," i.e. a Twomey signal, with small or countervailing water-path adjustments.[^2] In the forcing decomposition of [[yuan-2024-termination-shock]], the Twomey term contributes roughly 40% of the IMO-2020 forcing, with cloud-fraction adjustment making up the rest.[^3] The persistent uncertainty in aerosol forcing lives instead in the [[cloud-adjustments]] that follow droplet-number changes.
