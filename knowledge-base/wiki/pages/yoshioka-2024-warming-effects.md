# Yoshioka et al. 2024 — Warming effects of reduced sulfur emissions from shipping

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.5194/acp-24-13681-2024 (Atmos. Chem. Phys. 24, 13681) — local copy: `raw/yoshioka-2024-warming-effects.pdf`
**Date ingested:** 2026-08-09
**Type:** paper

## Summary

A large-ensemble coupled-model study of the [[imo-2020-regulation]] from the Leeds/Met Office group (Yoshioka, Grosvenor, Booth, Morice, Carslaw). Twelve-member ensemble pairs of HadGEM3-GC3.1-LL run 2015–2049 under two scenarios — SHIP20 (the real post-2020 sulfur cut) versus SHIP100 (no cut) — put the effective radiative forcing at 0.13 W/m², remarkably close to UKESM1's 0.139 ([[jordan-henry-2024-ukesm1]]) despite independent model lineage.[^1]

The distinctive contributions are the time-profile and the Arctic. Global mean warming averages 0.04 K over 2020–2049, but the ensemble shows the signal emerging from variability only in the late 2020s — "our simulations are not clear on whether the global impact is yet to emerge or has already emerged because the present-day impact is masked by variability."[^2] Warming is strongly amplified poleward: 0.15 K in the Arctic, up to 0.3 K in its Atlantic sector.[^3] For scale, the shipping-sulfur forcing equals roughly half of the net positive forcing from the reduction of *all* anthropogenic aerosols since the late 20th century.[^4]

On 2023, they bound the shipping contribution: combined effects "could potentially combine to explain up to 0.08 K of the 0.1 to 0.12 K of unexplained 2023 warming" — an upper bound, not an attribution.[^5]

## Key Takeaways

- ERF = 0.13 W/m² (HadGEM3, 12-member ensemble pairs, 2015–2049).[^1]
- Global warming response ~0.04 K (2020–2049 mean); Arctic 0.15 K, Atlantic-Arctic 0.3 K.[^3]
- Signal emerges from internal variability only in the late 2020s.[^2]
- Shipping cut ≈ 50% of the forcing from all recent anthropogenic aerosol decline.[^4]
