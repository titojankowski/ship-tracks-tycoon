# Yuan et al. 2024 — Abrupt reduction in shipping emission as an inadvertent geoengineering termination shock produces substantial radiative warming

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s43247-024-01442-3 (Communications Earth & Environment 5, 281) — local copy: `raw/yuan-2024-termination-shock.pdf`
**Date ingested:** 2026-08-09
**Type:** paper

## Summary

The high-end estimate of the [[imo-2020-regulation]]'s climate impact, and the paper that put "[[inadvertent-geoengineering]] termination shock" into the shipping-aerosol vocabulary. Yuan and colleagues combined NASA GEOS-GOCART chemical-transport simulations of the SO₂ cut with deep-learning estimates of cloud droplet number change and cloud-adjustment relationships derived from a large ship-track sample, then fed the resulting forcing into an energy-balance model.[^1] The result: "The regulation leads to a radiative forcing of +0.2±0.11 W m⁻² averaged over the global ocean."[^2] Decomposed, roughly 40% is the [[twomey-effect]], ~0% LWP adjustment, and 60% cloud-fraction adjustment.[^1]

The temperature claim is the provocative part: the forcing "could lead to a doubling (or more) of the warming rate in the 2020s compared with the rate since 1980" — an expected transient response of ~0.24 K/decade, with the North Atlantic taking the strongest hit (~0.56 W/m² basin mean, peaking at 1.4 W/m²).[^3] The forcing is equivalent to ~80% of the measured increase in planetary heat uptake since 2020.[^4]

The authors themselves flag major caveats: the modeled droplet-number change (0.5 cm⁻³) is tiny against background (28 cm⁻³) and unconstrained by observation; the post-2020 record is too short for trend attribution; and no feedbacks are included.[^5] Critics (see [[2023-warmth-attribution]]) argue the energy-balance approach overstates the fast response; [[jordan-henry-2024-ukesm1]] and [[gettelman-2024-brought-forward]] land 2–3× lower on forcing.

## Key Takeaways

- Forcing: +0.2 ± 0.11 W/m² over the global ocean; ~0.56 W/m² North Atlantic basin mean.[^2][^3]
- Composition: ~40% Twomey, ~60% cloud-fraction adjustment, ~0% LWP.[^1]
- Claims possible doubling of the 2020s warming rate vs the post-1980 rate.[^3]
- Frames IMO 2020 as a termination shock for an unintended geoengineering scheme; poses the air-quality-vs-warming trade-off as an open policy question.[^6]
- Self-declared caveats: unconstrained Nd change, short record, no feedbacks.[^5]
